We audited 10 of the best-known SEO sites. They're mostly spotless.

A polite, ~300-page crawl of each of Moz, Ahrefs, Semrush, Backlinko, Yoast, Search Engine Journal, Search Engine Roundtable, SE Ranking, Surfer and Neil Patel. Here is what even the experts occasionally miss, every example re-checked by hand, and every row of data free to download.

Seven examples, each re-checked by hand

We re-requested every headline example with curl, one request at a time, about an hour after the crawl. All seven still held. The full log is in data/evidence/curl-log.jsonl. We share these because they are useful, not because anyone did anything wrong: each one is a single-line fix.

backlinko.com · E1

A placeholder link that slipped through

On /pillar-pages, the anchor “strong network of internal links” points at a URL that looks like a page-builder placeholder. It returns 404. Ironic, and very human.

backlinko.com · E2

66 links that take a detour

Every one of the 66 internal URLs we found redirecting on Backlinko does so with a 301/308; 56 end on sister site searchengineland.com (both sites are owned by Semrush, so this looks like content moving between sister brands). Not broken, just an extra hop. Full list in backlinko-redirects.csv.

What we didn't count, and why

  • 177 bot-walled URLs (403 and similar, mostly on Moz and Backlinko). A firewall saying no to a crawler is not a broken page.
  • Long titles (633 pages over 60 characters). Google rewrites titles anyway; it is a style choice, not an error.
  • Near-duplicate pages (91) and cross-domain canonicals (e.g. SEJ's syndicated posts). Usually intentional.
  • Tracking pixels and 1×1 images without alt. They are not content.
  • Anything outside each site's blog/learn scope, and anything behind a login. Every exclusion is listed in excluded-flags.csv.

The honest caveats

  • ~300 pages is a sample, not a full audit. A different crawl order would find slightly different pages.
  • Toadstone is our tool. We built it, so we have a reason to want it to find things. That is why every rule, script and row is public.
  • These are some of the best-run sites on the web. 2,521 pages with 3 broken URLs is a seriously high bar, and the patterns here are the ones every large site has.

Method

  • When: crawls ran 23 Oct 2026, about 18:50–19:27 ET; examples re-verified 19:45–19:48 ET.
  • How much: up to 300 URLs per site, depth 5, starting from each site's blog or learn hub.
  • How politely: at most 2 requests per second per site, 0.5 s delay, concurrency 2, robots.txt respected, no logins, no cookies kept.
  • Who: ToadstoneBot/1.0 (+https://github.com/spcaeo/toadstone; polite research crawl, <=2 req/s)
  • What we kept: public page metadata only (status, title/meta/canonical/H1 presence, image alt counts, link status). No page text, headers or cookies.

Counting rules

  • Page checks apply only to HTML pages that returned 200.
  • Broken = 404, 410 or 5xx. 401/403/429 and timeouts are “blocked, not counted”.
  • Counted issues = no meta description + no canonical + no H1 + more than one H1 + pages with a content image missing alt + broken URLs + internally linked URLs that redirect.
  • Full definitions: DATA-DICTIONARY.md and the full methodology page (every exclusion, reproduce commands). Questions? See the FAQ.
